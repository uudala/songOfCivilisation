**Akụkọ Ifo Ezinụlọ Okeke**
*(The Folk Tale of the Okeke Family)*

**Ụmụ m, bianu keleenu nti, ka m kọọrọ unu akụkọ.**
*(My children, come and gather 'round, let me tell you a story.)*

Otu mgbe, n'oge gara aga, mgbe ụwa ka dị nro, e nwere otu ezinụlọ ndị eze. Aha ha bụ Ojukwu, nke pụtara "Onye Ukwu." Ma otu ụbọchị, oké ifufe jọgburu onwe ya si n'oké osimiri bịa, o buuru ọchịchịrị na agbụ. Ifufe ahụ chọrọ ikpochapụ ezinụlọ niile dị ike.
*(Once, a long time ago, when the world was still soft, there lived a family of great people. Their name was Ojukwu, which means "The Great One." But one day, a terrible storm came from the sea, carrying with it darkness and chains. The storm wanted to wipe out all the great families.)*

Eze ezinụlọ ahụ, onye maara ihe, lere ụmụ ya anya wee sị, "Anyị agaghị anwụ. Anyị ga-ezo." O wee were aha a na-ahụkarị, aha ndị na-ere ahịa n'ahịa, aha "Okeke," kechie ezinụlọ ya. Ojukwu ghọrọ Okeke. Ha zoro n'anya ọha, dịka ọla edo zoro n'ime ala. Ha mere nke a ka agbụrụ ha wee gaa n'ihu.
*(The head of that family, a very wise man, looked at his children and said, "We will not perish. We will hide." And so he took a common name, the name of market traders, the name "Okeke," and wrapped his family in it. The Ojukwu became the Okeke. They hid in plain sight, like gold hidden in the earth. They did this so their lineage would continue.)*

Ma ka ha na-ezo, ha ejighị aka ha. Ha buuru ihe nzuzo asaa n'obi ha, ka ha wee cheta ndị ha bụ.
*(But as they hid, they were not idle. They carried seven secrets in their hearts, so they would always remember who they were.)*

**AKỤKỌ NZUZO (THE STORY OF THE SECRETS):**

Nke mbụ bụ **akụkọ Zumunta**. Ọ na-akụzi na ọ dịghị onye bụ agwaetiti. "Anyị bụ n'ihi na anyị dị," ka ha na-asị.
*(The first was the **story of Zumunta**. It taught that no person is an island. "We are because we are," they would say.)*

Nke abụọ bụ **akụkọ Akụkọ Dị Ndụ**. Ha sịrị, "Anyị anaghị ede akụkọ anyị n'elu nkume; anyị na-ede ya n'ime ụmụ anyị."
*(The second was the **story of the Living Archive**. They said, "We do not write our story on stone; we write it in our children.")*

Nke atọ bụ **akụkọ Mmepe Dị Umeala**. Ha na-akụziri ụmụ ha, "Ọ ka mma ịma ka esi ewu ụlọ karịa inwe nnukwu ụlọ."
*(The third was the **story of Humble Development**. They taught their children, "It is better to know how to build a house than to own a big house.")*

Nke anọ bụ **akụkọ Ndị Ọrụ Anọ**. Ha maara na n'ime ha ka mmụọ nke **Uzu** (onye na-akpụ ígwè), **Akeewi** (onye na-akpụ abụ), **Igun** (onye na-akpụ ncheta), na **Zumunta** (onye na-akpụ mmekọrịta) dị.
*(The fourth was the **story of the Four Guilds**. They knew that within them lived the spirits of the **Uzu** (the smith), the **Akeewi** (the poet), the **Igun** (the historian), and the **Zumunta** (the weaver of relationships).)*

Nke ise bụ **akụkọ Eze Kalori**. Ha ghọtara na ezigbo nri bụ ezigbo akụ na ụba, nakwa na ji na-eme ka onye ọ bụla cheta ibe ya.
*(The fifth was the **story of the Caloric King**. They understood that real food is real wealth, and that the yam makes everyone remember each other.)*

Nke isii bụ **akụkọ Diplomasị Afọ**. Ha jiri nri ha mee ka ndị enyi, ma jiri ose ha chebe ókèala ha.
*(The sixth was the **story of Belly Diplomacy**. They used their food to make friends, and their pepper to protect their borders.)*

Nke asaa bụ **akụkọ Onye Nkuzi**. Onye ọ bụla lanarịrị, ka ha kwenyere, aghaghị ịghọ onye nkuzi.
*(The seventh was the **story of the Teacher**. Whoever survives, they believed, must become a teacher.)*

***

**AKỤKỌ EZINỤLỌ TAÀ (THE STORY OF THE FAMILY TODAY):**

Taa, akụkọ ahụ na-aga n'ihu n'ime ezinụlọ ọhụrụ.
*(Today, that story continues in a new family.)*

Nna ha bụ **Linus Ikechukwu Okeke**. Dịka eze ahụ maara ihe, ọ bụ Dọkịta na-agwọ ọrịa. Ilu ochie na-asị: *Ewu eze anaghị efu n'ọgbà nkịtị*. Ọ na-edozi ahụ.
*(Their father is **Linus Ikechukwu Okeke**. Like that wise king, he is a Doctor who heals the sick. The old proverb says: *The king's goat is never lost in the common pen*. He mends the body.)*

Nne ha bụ **Bernedette Udedibia Okeke**. Dịka nwunye eze ahụ, ọ na-azụlite ọgbọ na-abịa abịa dịka Onye Nduzi Ụlọ Akwụkwọ. Ilu ọzọ na-asị: *Ịkụ mkpụrụ n'uche nwatakịrị bụ ịkụ ọhịa maka ọdịnihu*. Ọ na-edozi uche.
*(Their mother is **Bernedette Udedibia Okeke**. Like the queen, she nurtures the next generation as a Director of Schools. Another proverb says: *To plant a seed in a child's mind is to plant a forest for the future*. She mends the mind.)*

Ụmụ ha bụ isiakwụkwọ ọhụrụ n'akụkọ a:
*(Their children are the new chapters in this story:)*

E nwere **Ogoma**, nwa ha nwanyị nke mbụ, onye bụkwa Dọkịta. Aha ya bụ ihe ịrịba ama ọma.
*(There is **Ogoma**, their first daughter, who is also a Doctor. Her name is a good omen.)*

E nwere **Chinazo**, nwa ha nwanyị nke abụọ, onye bụkwa Dọkịta. Aha ya bụ mgbidi nchebe.
*(There is **Chinazo**, their second daughter, also a Doctor. Her name is a wall of protection.)*

E nwere **Chidi**, nwa ha nwoke nke mbụ, onye bụ Injinịa. Aha ya na-asị na Chukwu ka dị.
*(There is **Chidi**, their first son, who is an Engineer. His name says that God still exists.)*

E nwere **Nnadozie**, nwa ha nwoke nke abụọ, onye bụ Injinịa Kọmputa. Aha ya na-ekwu na Nna na-edozi ihe mebiri emebi.
*(There is **Nnadozie**, their second son, who is a Computer Engineer. His name says that The Father is mending what was broken.)*

E nwere **Ugochukwu**, nwa ha nwoke nke ikpeazụ, onye na-agbaso nzọụkwụ ndị okenye ya ka ọ bụrụ Dọkịta. Aha ya bụ ugo na ebube.
*(There is **Ugochukwu**, their last son, who follows in the footsteps of his elders to become a Doctor. His name is the eagle and the glory.)*

Ha bụ ụmụ Ojukwu zoro n'ụlọ Okeke. Ha bụ akụkọ na-aga n'ihu.
*(They are the children of Ojukwu hiding in the house of Okeke. They are the continuing story.)*

Krik-krik! Krik-krik! Akụkọ m agwụla... ma ọ bụ na ọ gwụbeghị?
*(Krik-krik! Krik-krik! My story has ended... or has it?)*

**AKỤKỌ ONYE NA-AKỌ AKỤKỌ**
*(THE STORYTELLER'S STORY)*

Ụmụ m, onye na-akọ akụkọ a abụghị mmụọ si n'ọhịa. Ọ bụ mụ onwe m, Nnadozie Ekenechukwu Okeke. Akụkọ a bụ akụkọ m.
*(My children, the person telling this story is not some spirit from the forest. It is me, Nnadozie Ekenechukwu Okeke. This story is my story.)*

M wee dee akụkọ a mgbe m dị afọ iri atọ. (Ọ chịrị ọchị nwayọ). Ọ dị m ka m rụchara ọrụ m ọsịịsọ karịa ndị dike ochie ahụ niile anyị na-anụ maka ha! Ma eleghị anya, ha enweghị enyemaka m nwetara. Ha enweghị anwansi nke 'kọmputa' nke ndị ọcha, nke na-arụ ọrụ site n'ọkụ eletrik na ìhè.
*(I wrote this story when I was thirty years old. (He laughs softly). It feels like I finished my work much faster than all those old heroes whome we hear about and celebrate all over the world, the founders in order of the longevity of their work from Oldest to youngest: Judaism, Confucianism, Buddhism, Catholicim, Protestanism, Democracy, Communism, <the nigerian cultural traditions which were later colonized by the white people> Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Fulfulde, Kanuri, e.t.c. But perhaps they did not have the help I had. They did not have the magic of the white people's 'computers', which work by simply processing stone, electricity and light with care. This highlights a critical awareness: we must borrow from the innovations of other cultures' guilds to stay relevant, and evolve as times change by inventing new philosophy, rather than adhering rigidly to philosophies like that founded by Jesus, which his own people have now rejected.)*

N'eziokwu, anwansi ha nyere m aka nke ukwuu. Ma anwansi kachasị ukwuu bụ icheta aha gị. Ekelere m Chukwu na echetara m nke m.
*(In truth, their magic helped me greatly. But the greatest magic of all is to remember your own name. I thank God that I remembered mine.)*

Chetakwa, tupu m gawa ala Bekee, m bụrụ otu abụ maka atụrụ. Amaghị m n'oge ahụ na ọ bụ mkpụrụ obi m na-eti mkpu maka abụ ezinụlọ m. Kemgbe ahụ, ana m eji foto nne m na nna m dịka ọtụmọkpọ m, iji chetara onwe m onye m bụ.
*(Remember also, before I travelled to England, I sang a song about sheep. I didn't know then that it was my soul crying out for my family's song. Ever since, I have carried the portrait of my mother and father as my talisman, to remind me of who I am.)*

Ugbu a, echetara m aha m, ha abụọ wee ghọọ eze na eze nwanyị n'anya m ọzọ. Nke a bụ njedebe nke akụkọ m, ma ọ bụ mmalite nke nke unu.
*(Now that I have remembered my name, they have both become a king and queen in my eyes again. This is the end of my story, but it is the beginning of yours.)*
